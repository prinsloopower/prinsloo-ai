# QBR prep

A QBR reports against the **customer's goals** in the customer's units: hours saved, revenue gained, errors avoided. Product usage is evidence for those results, never the headline.

## Gather
- The goals the customer set, from the contract, kickoff notes or the last review.
- Results and usage for the period, from connected tools or exports.
- Support history: tickets, incidents, escalations, and what was done about them.
- Renewal date, contract value, stakeholders.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

## Steps
Build two documents.

**Customer-facing review**
1. Their goals, in their words.
2. Scorecard: each goal with its target, the result and the trend. Where a goal is not yet measured, say so and give the plan to measure it.
3. Value delivered, in their units, with the calculation shown.
4. Adoption: who uses what, and where usage is thin.
5. Issues: what went wrong this period, what was fixed, what is still open. Include them before the customer raises them.
6. Their next period: upcoming priorities, as far as the user knows them, phrased as questions to confirm.
7. Mutual action plan: actions on both sides with owners and dates.
8. The ask, placed last: renewal, expansion, a reference.

**Internal prep sheet**
- Renewal date and value, health, risks.
- Stakeholder map: sponsor, champion, detractor, anyone new.
- Hard questions to expect, with answers.
- What the user wants from the meeting.

Offer to build the review as slides or a document with the session's tools.

## Done when
- Every goal has a result, or "not yet measured" with a plan.
- The issues section is present.
- The mutual plan has owners on both sides.
- Internal notes are separate from anything the customer will see.
