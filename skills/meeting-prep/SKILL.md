# Meeting prep

A **brief** fits on one page and is read in the ten minutes before the meeting. It ends with what the user wants to walk out with.

## Gather
- The invite: time, attendees, agenda. From the connected calendar, or pasted.
- History with these people: earlier threads, notes, documents, account records, from connected tools or supplied by the user.
- For external attendees and companies: public role, background and news from the last 90 days, by web search when available.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

## Steps
1. State the user's goal in one sentence: the decision, commitment or information they want by the end. Ask if it cannot be inferred from the invite and history.
2. Profile each attendee in two lines: role, what they care about in this meeting, last interaction with the user.
3. Summarize the history as a dated list of what has been agreed and what is still open.
4. Predict three asks or objections and write the user's response to each, with the supporting fact.
5. Write three to five questions the user should ask.
6. Flag sensitive points: unresolved complaints, promises that slipped, topics one attendee has asked to keep off the table.
7. Add logistics only where they affect the meeting: who presents, what to bring, hard stops.

## Shape
```
<Meeting>, <date and time>
Your goal: <one sentence>
In the room: <name, role, cares about, last contact>
Where things stand: <dated list>
Expect: <ask or objection> -> <your response>
Ask them: <questions>
Sensitive: <points>
```

## Done when
- The brief is under 400 words.
- The goal is one sentence.
- Every statement about a person or company carries a source, or is marked `unverified`.
- Details about people are professional and public. Personal details stay out.
