# Competitor research

Every line in the profile is **sourced** and dated, and labeled by kind: **fact** (the competitor's own site, filings, documentation), **reported** (press, reviews, analysts) or **inference** (the reasoning shown).

## Gather
- The competitor or competitors, and why the question is being asked now: a deal, a launch, planning.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.
- Internal knowledge the user can share: win and loss notes, sales feedback, earlier profiles.
- The public web, by search. With no web access, work from supplied material and list what could not be checked.

## Steps
1. Snapshot: what they sell, to whom, and size signals such as headcount, funding, revenue and locations.
2. Positioning: how they describe themselves, quoted from their own pages, and who they say they are for.
3. Product: main capabilities, recent releases, and gaps visible from documentation and reviews.
4. Pricing and packaging: published prices and tiers. Where pricing is private, write "not public" with any reported figures and their source.
5. Customers: named customers, segments, case studies.
6. Go-to-market: channels, partners, hiring patterns.
7. Recent moves in the last 12 months: funding, launches, leadership changes, acquisitions, layoffs. Date each one.
8. Strengths and weaknesses, each with its evidence. From review sites, report themes with counts.
9. What it means for us: where we overlap, where they threaten, where they leave an opening.
10. List the sources with the date each was accessed. Mark anything older than 12 months.

For several competitors, write one profile each and add a comparison table on the same rows.

## Done when
- Every section is filled, or reads "not found" with what was searched.
- Every claim carries a source and a label of fact, reported or inference.
- "What it means for us" refers to the user's own products and customers.
- The profile opens with a five-line summary and the research date.
