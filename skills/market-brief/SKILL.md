# Market brief

Size the market twice: **top-down** from published figures and **bottom-up** from counts multiplied by price. The gap between the two answers is a finding.

## Gather
- The market, with its boundaries: product category, customer type, geography, time frame.
- Why the user is asking: entry decision, fundraising, planning. This sets the depth.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.
- Published sources by web search: statistics offices, regulators, trade bodies, company filings, analyst summaries. With no web access, work from supplied material and list what could not be checked.

Ask once, in one message, if the boundaries are unclear. A brief on the wrong market is wasted.

## Steps
1. Define the market: what is in, what is out, and the adjacent markets left aside.
2. Size it. Top-down: cited totals narrowed step by step to the served market. Bottom-up: number of buyers, times adoption, times annual spend, with the arithmetic run in code and shown. State **TAM**, **SAM** and **SOM** with the method behind each.
3. Growth: historical rate, forecasts and what drives them.
4. Segments: by customer type, size, need or region, with relative size.
5. Buyers: who decides, who pays, how they buy, what they switch from.
6. Competition: the main players, concentration, and how new entrants have fared.
7. Trends and regulation: what is changing, and rules that shape entry or cost.
8. Risks and unknowns.
9. Implications for the user's question, and what to find out next.

## Done when
- Both sizing methods are shown, or the missing one is explained.
- Every figure names its publisher, its year and what it measures.
- Conflicting estimates appear as a range with both sources.
- Market definitions stay the same from the first section to the last.
- The brief opens with a five-line summary.
