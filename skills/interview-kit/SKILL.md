# Interview kit

A **structured interview** asks every candidate the same questions and scores each answer against written **anchors**: what a weak, a solid and an excellent answer contain. The kit makes interviewers comparable.

## Gather
- The job description, or the role's outcomes and must-haves.
- Interview stages and who is on the panel.
- Level, and anything the team has learned from past hires in this role.

## Steps
1. Derive four to six **competencies** from the must-haves and outcomes. Define each in one line.
2. For each competency write two behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time when..."), one situational question ("What would you do if..."), and probes that draw out the situation, the candidate's own actions and the result.
3. Write the rubric for each competency: scores 1 to 4, each with an anchor describing what an answer at that level contains.
4. Design one work sample that mirrors real work in the role, with instructions, a time limit and its own rubric.
5. Build the interview plan: each stage, its interviewer, its competencies and its length. Each competency is covered once.
6. Write the scorecard: a score and evidence per competency, then an overall recommendation.
7. Add the panel rules: same questions for every candidate, scores submitted before the debrief, evidence quoted from the candidate's answers.
8. Keep every question tied to a listed competency. Questions about age, family, health, origin, religion and other personal circumstances stay out of the kit.

## Shape
```
Competencies | name | definition | from which must-have |
Per competency: questions, probes, anchors for 1 to 4
Work sample: brief, time limit, rubric
Plan | stage | interviewer | competencies | minutes |
Scorecard
Panel rules
```

## Done when
- Every must-have in the job description is covered by a competency.
- Every competency has questions and four anchors.
- Each competency is assigned to exactly one stage.
- Every question traces to a competency.
