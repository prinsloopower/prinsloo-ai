# Account brief

Look for **trigger events**: changes at the account in the last six months that create a reason to talk now. The brief connects each one to something the user can offer.

## Gather
- The account name, and what the user is preparing for.
- Our history: deals, contracts, support tickets, emails, call notes, from connected tools or supplied. Where nothing is found, write "no record found".
- The public picture by web search: their site, filings, news, job postings, leadership profiles. With no web access, work from supplied material and say what could not be checked.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

## Steps
1. Snapshot: what they do, size, markets, business model, ownership.
2. Trigger events, each dated: leadership changes, funding, earnings, restructuring, launches, expansion, hiring surges, regulatory pressure.
3. Likely priorities: three, each tied to the evidence that suggests it.
4. People: for each contact the user will meet, role, tenure, professional background and what the role usually cares about. Public and professional information only.
5. Our history: what they bought, what they use, open issues, last contact, who on our side knows them.
6. Whitespace: what they could use from us and do not have.
7. Openers: three questions that connect a trigger event to a problem we solve.
8. Risks: competitors in the account, a champion who left, unresolved complaints.

## Shape
```
<Account>, prepared <date> for <purpose>
In five lines
Snapshot | What's changed | Likely priorities
People   | Our history    | Whitespace
Ask them | Risks | Sources
```

## Done when
- Every priority cites the event or statement behind it.
- Every external fact carries a source and a date.
- There are three opening questions.
- The history section draws only on the user's own records.
