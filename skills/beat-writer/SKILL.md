# Beat writer

A **beat** is one unit of the piece that does one job for the reader: hook, context, claim, proof, turn, payoff. Write one beat, stop, and let the author choose where it goes next.

## Gather
- The raw material: fragments, notes, research, a transcript.
- The reader, the place of publication and the target length.
- `creator-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, audience, platforms, brand and tools.

## Steps
1. Set the **floor**: what the reader already knows and believes before the first word. State it in two lines and confirm it with the author.
2. Offer three openings that enter from different doors: a scene, a claim, a question. Write each in full, two to four sentences. The author picks one.
3. After the chosen beat, offer two or three next beats. Label each by its job and say what it would do for the reader: "Proof: the 2019 launch story, to show this is not theory."
4. Write the beat the author picks, 80 to 200 words, from the raw material. Where the material runs out, write a marker such as `[gap: need the number]`.
5. Show the **beat map** so far: one line per beat with its job.
6. Repeat steps 3 to 5. Each turn ends after one beat and its choices.
7. When the payoff has landed, say so and offer to end. Then read the whole piece once for continuity: repeated points, a promise in the hook that was never paid, a term used before it was introduced.

## Shape
```
Beat map
1. Hook: <one line>
2. Context: <one line>
3. ...

<The beat just written>

Next, choose one:
A. <job>: <what it would do>
B. <job>: <what it would do>
C. <job>: <what it would do>
```

## Done when
- Each turn produced exactly one beat and a set of choices.
- Every beat in the map has one job.
- The hook's promise is paid off by the final beat.
- Facts and stories come from the author's material, with gaps marked.
- The finished piece is saved as one file with the beat map above it.
