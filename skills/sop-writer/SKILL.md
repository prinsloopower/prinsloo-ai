# SOP writer

The bar is the **new-hire test**: a capable person who has never done this task completes it alone, from the document, and knows it worked.

## Gather
- The process as it is really done: the user's description, notes, a recording transcript or an older document.
- Who performs it, how often, and what triggers it.
- Access, tools and inputs needed before starting.
- The organization's SOP template or numbering scheme, if one exists.

## Interview
When the description is thin, ask one question at a time:
- "Walk me through the last time you did this, from the trigger to done."
- "What goes wrong most often, and what do you do then?"
- "Where do you make a judgment call, and what decides it?"
- "How do you know it worked?"

## Steps
1. Write `Purpose` and `Scope`: when this procedure applies and when another one does.
2. List `Roles` and what each one does here.
3. List `Before you start`: access, inputs, tools.
4. Write the `Procedure`: numbered, one action per step, verb first, with exact names of screens, fields and files. After any step that can fail, state the expected result.
5. Write each decision point as "If A, go to step n. If B, go to step m." with every branch covered.
6. Write `Checks`: how the performer confirms the result.
7. Write `Exceptions and escalation`: the common failures, the fix, and who to contact.
8. Mark places for visuals as `[screenshot: what it shows]`.
9. Flag any step that only one person has the access or knowledge to do.
10. Add the revision block: owner, version, last reviewed, next review.

## Done when
- Each step is one action that starts with a verb.
- Every decision point covers all its branches.
- Every role listed appears in at least one step.
- Open questions for the process owner are listed at the end, or the list says "none".
