# Deck storyline

A **ghost deck** is the titles with no slides yet. Each one is an **action title**: a full sentence that states the slide's conclusion. Read in order, the titles alone make the argument.

## Gather
- Audience, time slot, and whether the deck is presented live or sent to be read.
- What the audience should decide, believe or do afterwards.
- The material: data, analysis, earlier decks, notes.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

Ask once, in one message, if the audience or the wanted outcome is missing.

## Steps
1. Write the governing message in one sentence: what the audience should leave with.
2. Choose the arc. **SCQA** (situation, complication, question, answer) to persuade. Answer first, then reasons, for a decision-maker short on time. Past, present, next for a review.
3. Write one action title per slide. "Churn fell 18% after the onboarding redesign", in place of "Churn update".
4. Under each title, name the proof: the chart, table, quote or example that makes the title true, and whether the data is in hand or `[needed]`.
5. Read the titles top to bottom as a paragraph. Reorder, merge or cut until it flows.
6. Fit the slot: about two minutes a slide when presented. Move everything else to an appendix.
7. Add the opening and the close: the first sentence spoken, and the ask on the last slide.

## Shape
```
Governing message: <one sentence>
| # | Action title | Proof | Visual | Data |
| 1 | ...          | ...   | bar chart | in hand |
Appendix: <backup slides>
```

Stop at the storyline. Offer to build the slides with the session's presentation tools once the user approves it.

## Done when
- The titles alone read as a complete argument.
- Every slide has one message and a named proof.
- The slide count fits the time slot.
- Every `[needed]` item is listed at the end with who could supply it.
