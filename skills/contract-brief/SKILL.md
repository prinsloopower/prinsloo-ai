# Contract brief

Every line of the brief cites its **clause**. The brief is a business reader's summary: it tells the user what they are agreeing to and what to ask a lawyer, and it says so at the top.

## Gather
- The full agreement with all schedules, exhibits and amendments. Name any that are referred to and missing.
- Which party the user is.
- The user's standard terms or the previous version, if a comparison is wanted.

## Steps
1. Read every page. State the page count and the documents covered.
2. `At a glance`: parties, type of agreement, effective date, term, total value.
3. `Money`: fees, payment terms, price increases, late charges, penalties, what triggers extra cost.
4. `Term and exit`: initial term, renewal mechanics, termination rights for each side, notice periods, what survives termination.
5. `Key dates`: a table of date, event and action needed. Compute the last day to give notice before any automatic renewal.
6. `Our obligations` and `Their obligations`: what each side must do, with service levels and remedies.
7. `Risk clauses`: liability caps and exclusions, indemnities, intellectual property ownership, confidentiality, data protection, exclusivity, restrictions on hiring or competing, assignment and change of control, governing law and disputes.
8. `Unusual or missing`: terms that differ from what is common for this type of agreement, and common protections that are absent. Where the contract does not address a topic, write "silent".
9. `Questions for legal`: specific questions, most important first.
10. For a comparison, add a table of each difference with both wordings.

Follow defined terms to their definitions, and note any conflict between the main body and a schedule.

## Done when
- Every statement cites a clause number and quotes the operative words.
- The key dates table includes every notice deadline, computed.
- All documents and the page count are named, with any missing exhibit flagged.
- The first line reads: "Business summary for review. Not legal advice."
