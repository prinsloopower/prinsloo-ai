# Decision memo

Depth follows **reversibility**. A two-way door, cheap to undo, gets half a page. A one-way door gets every section below.

## Gather
- The decision to be made, who makes it, and the deadline.
- The options already under discussion and the constraints: budget, time, policy.
- Evidence: data, quotes, earlier analysis, from connected tools or supplied.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

## Steps
1. State the decision as one question, with the decision-maker and the date.
2. Give the context a newcomer needs, in under 100 words.
3. Set the criteria and their order of importance before describing any option.
4. Lay out at least three options, one of them **do nothing**. For each: what it is, cost, benefit, main risk, how reversible, and who would favor it and why.
5. Compare the options against the criteria in one table.
6. Recommend one option, with the reasoning traced to the criteria.
7. State what would have to be true for the recommendation to be wrong.
8. Add the decision record. Fill it if the user supplies the outcome, otherwise leave it for the decision-maker.

## Shape
```
Decision needed: <question>. Decider: <name>. By: <date>.
Context
Criteria (ranked)
Options | option | cost | benefit | risk | reversible? |
Recommendation
We would be wrong if...
Decision record: decision | date | decided by | reasoning | dissent | revisit when
```

## Done when
- There are at least three options, including do nothing, and each is one a reasonable person might choose.
- The recommendation follows from the ranked criteria.
- The decision record block is present.
- Every number, name and date traces to the inputs. A gap shows as a bracketed placeholder such as `[cost?]`.
