# Battlecard

A battlecard is one page a rep reads in the two minutes before a call. Every line is something they can say out loud, and every claim about the competitor can be backed up if the buyer challenges it.

## Gather
- A competitor profile: output of competitor research, or sources the user supplies.
- Win and loss notes, call notes, objections reps actually hear.
- The user's proof: customer names cleared for use, numbers, case studies.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

With no competitor material at all, research the competitor first and say that the card rests on public sources only.

## Steps
1. Their pitch in one line, in their words.
2. **Where we win**: three points, each with a proof point a rep can cite.
3. **Where we lose**: three honest points, each with how to respond or when to walk away.
4. **Landmines to set**: questions for the buyer that lead them to discover the competitor's weakness themselves.
5. **Landmines they set**: what the competitor says about us, and the response.
6. Objections: the buyer's sentence, then the rep's reply in under 40 words.
7. Pricing talk track: how the two models compare and how to frame total cost.
8. Proof: references and switch stories.
9. Footnote the source and date for each claim about the competitor, and add a `Last verified` date.

## Shape
```
vs <Competitor>. Last verified <date>
Their pitch
Where we win  | point | proof |
Where we lose | point | response |
Ask the buyer
They will say -> you say
Objections
Pricing
Proof
```

## Done when
- The card fits one page, about 350 words.
- Every "where we win" has a proof point, and "where we lose" has three entries.
- Every claim about the competitor is footnoted, accurate and fair. Unverifiable claims are left off the card.
- Read aloud, each line sounds like speech.
