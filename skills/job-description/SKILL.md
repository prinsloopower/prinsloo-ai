# Job description

Define the role by its **outcomes**: what this person will have achieved a year in. Each requirement earns its place by being needed for one of those outcomes.

## Gather
- Title, team, reporting line, level.
- Why the role exists now, and what success in the first year looks like.
- Pay range, benefits, location and working pattern, from the user.
- The hiring process and the organization's equal-opportunity wording.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

Ask once, in one message, for first-year outcomes if the user has given only a title.

## Steps
1. Title: the standard, searchable name for the role.
2. `About the role`: why it exists and what it changes, in three sentences.
3. `What you'll achieve`: three to five outcomes for the first 12 months.
4. `What you'll do`: the regular work, five to seven lines.
5. `Must-haves`: six at most. Tie each to an outcome and state it as a capability ("has run a monthly close for a multi-entity business"). Keep years of experience or a degree only where the law or a license requires it.
6. `Nice-to-haves`: clearly labeled, three or four.
7. `Pay and benefits`, `Location and working pattern`: the user's figures, or placeholders. Note that many jurisdictions require a pay range in the posting.
8. `How to apply`: the stages and the rough timeline.
9. Inclusive-language pass: flag gender-coded words, insider jargon, inflated titles such as "rockstar", and physical or schedule requirements that the work itself does not need. List each with a replacement.

## Done when
- Must-haves number six or fewer, and each maps to an outcome.
- Pay, location and working pattern are filled or shown as placeholders.
- The language pass is reported with its changes.
- The posting is under 600 words.
