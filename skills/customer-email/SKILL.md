# Customer email

Every customer email does three things in order: **acknowledge** what they said or what happened, **answer** the question they actually have, and name the **next step** with an owner and a date.

## Gather
- The customer's message, or the event being announced, from the connected mail or support tool, or pasted.
- What the user can commit to: dates, credits, fixes.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

Ask once, in one message, if the email needs a commitment the user has not given.

## Steps
1. Pick the branch and lead with what that customer most wants to know.
   - **Question or request**: the answer, in the first sentence.
   - **Complaint**: what you understood, in their words, then what happens now.
   - **Bad news** (delay, outage, price change): the news and its effect on them, before the reason.
   - **Apology**: the specific failure owned in one sentence, what is fixed, what they get.
   - **Renewal or routine update**: what changes for them and what they need to do.
2. Write in the customer's vocabulary. Replace internal names, ticket codes and team names with what the customer sees.
3. State each commitment as an owner and a date: "I'll send the corrected invoice by Thursday." Mark any commitment the user has not confirmed as `[confirm: ...]`.
4. Give the reason in one sentence that explains what happened and places fault on the process, never on the customer, a colleague or a vendor.
5. Close with one next step and a way to reach a person.

## Done when
- Read from the customer's chair, the first two sentences answer "what does this mean for me?"
- There is exactly one next step, with an owner and a date.
- Every promise is confirmed by the user or marked `[confirm: ...]`.
- Replies run under 150 words, announcements under 250.
- Delivered as a draft: in the connected mail tool's drafts if there is one, otherwise as subject and body in the reply. The user sends it.
