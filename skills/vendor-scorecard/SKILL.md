# Vendor scorecard

The **weights** are agreed before any vendor is scored. That order is what makes the result an evaluation and not a justification.

## Gather
- The need: what the purchase must do, for whom, by when, within what budget.
- The candidates, with proposals, quotes, documentation, demo notes and reference calls.
- Constraints that rule a vendor out: security, compliance, integration, location.
- Public information by web search where the user's material has gaps.

## Steps
1. Split requirements into **must-haves** (pass or fail) and **weighted criteria**. Typical criteria: fit to need, ease of use, integration, security, support, vendor stability, cost.
2. Propose weights that add up to 100 and confirm them with the user before scoring.
3. Test each vendor against the must-haves. A fail removes the vendor, with the reason recorded.
4. Score each remaining vendor 1 to 5 on each criterion, with one line of evidence and its source per score. Where evidence is missing, leave the score blank and write the question for the vendor.
5. Compute weighted totals in a spreadsheet or code.
6. Build total cost of ownership over three years: licenses, implementation, training, internal time, switching and exit.
7. Run sensitivity: move the heaviest weight up and down by 10 points and note whether the winner changes.
8. Recommend a vendor and a runner-up, state what would change the result, and list questions for demos, references and negotiation.

Deliver the scorecard as a spreadsheet when the session can build one, with the summary in the reply.

## Done when
- Weights add up to 100 and were confirmed before scoring.
- Every score has evidence, and every blank has a question.
- Any vendor failing a must-have is marked out with the reason.
- Three-year cost and the sensitivity result are shown.
