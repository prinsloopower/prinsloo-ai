# Executive email

An executive reads the first two lines on a phone between meetings. Write **BLUF**: bottom line up front, everything after it optional reading.

## Gather
- The ask: decide, approve, unblock, or just know. One email carries one ask.
- The deadline, and what happens if it passes.
- Source material from the connected mail or document tools, or pasted by the user.
- `business-context.md`, if one is in the working folder, project or attachments: it sets voice, brand, products, customers and competitors.

Ask once, in one message, if the ask or the deadline is missing. Nothing else blocks a draft.

## Steps
1. Write the subject as the ask with its date, prefixed `Decision`, `Approval`, `Help needed` or `FYI`.
2. Line one states the ask and the deadline. Line two states the recommendation and its single strongest reason.
3. Add at most three bullets of context: cost, risk of waiting, alternative considered. Each bullet carries a number or a name.
4. Close with the exact reply that unblocks the sender: "Reply 'approved' and I'll sign today."
5. Move everything else to an attachment, or to a `Background` block below the sign-off.

## Shape
```
Subject: Decision needed by <date>: <the ask in eight words>

<Name>,

<Ask and deadline.> <Recommendation and strongest reason.>

- <Cost or key number>
- <Risk of waiting>
- <Alternative considered, and why not>

<The exact reply that unblocks you.>

<Sign-off>
```

## Done when
- The first two lines alone are enough to act on.
- The body above the sign-off is under 120 words.
- Every number, name and date traces to the inputs. A gap shows as a bracketed placeholder such as `[cost?]`.
- Delivered as a draft: in the connected mail tool's drafts if there is one, otherwise as subject and body in the reply. The user sends it.
